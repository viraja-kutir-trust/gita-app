## 1. Provenance

### A. Swami Swarupananda, *Srimad-Bhagavad-Gita* (1909)
- **Edition used:** FIRST EDITION, 1909, "Himalayan Series No. XX", printed by Mohan Lal Sah Chowdhari and
  published by the Prabuddha Bharata Press, Mayavati, Almora (title page in the scan). Preface dated
  "Advaita Ashrama, Mayavati, 18th October, 1909".
- **Scan:** archive.org `{SW_ID}` (Digital Library of India 2015.386852; City Central Library, Hyderabad;
  414 leaves, bitonal 300 dpi). The DLI metadata wrongly says "language: Telugu"; the book is English + Sanskrit.
- **Scan defects:** the page-number map of the scan shows printed pages missing from this copy after PDF pages
  {missing_pages}. Verses on those pages are **not** in the output (listed in section 4); we did not fill them
  from other editions.
- **Authorship note:** the Preface to the First Edition (signed "The Editors and Publishers") says the work was
  compiled by Swami Swarupananda with the collaboration of his brother sannyasins at Mayavati and some Western
  disciples of Swami Vivekananda (manuscript 1901–1903), and was published only after his death (1906), first
  serially in *Prabuddha Bharata*, then "carefully edited and enlarged with additional comments". Some comments
  are therefore editorial (Advaita Ashrama); credit as "Swami Swarupananda (Advaita Ashrama, 1909)".
- **Other scans checked:** `in.ernet.dli.2015.548149` = "Twelfth Revised Edition, September 1976" (different
  layout); `in.ernet.dli.2015.237563` and `bhagavad-gita-swarupananda-rkm` = "Tenth Impression, 1967";
  `BhagavadGitaTranslatedBySwamiSwarupananda` and `aGEn_srimad-bhagavad-gita-english-trans.-by-swami-swarupananda-advaita-ashrama-calcutta`
  = later Advaita Ashrama reprints (the latter spells "Krsna", i.e. revised). None is the 1909 edition. The 1967
  impression (djvu text of `bhagavad-gita-swarupananda-rkm` and `BhagavadGitaTranslatedBySwamiSwarupananda`) is
  used **only** as a witness to repair individual mis-read English gloss words — never to add text.
- **sacred-texts.com** (`hin/sbg`, read from the mirror neonvagabond.xyz): labelled "[1909]", but its page numbers
  (2.47 on p. 57) match the later Advaita Ashrama impressions (1909 has 2.47 on p. 49), and its wording differs from
  the 1909 print in places (e.g. 6.5 — 1909: "Let a man uplift himself by the Self. Let him not drag himself
  down…"; sacred-texts: "A man should uplift himself by his own self, so let him not weaken this self…"). It is
  used only to correct OCR mis-readings where the 1909 scan evidently has the same words; where the 1909 scan shows
  other real words the 1909 reading is kept (such verses are `medium`).
- **Licence:** public domain (published 1909; Swarupananda d. 1906).

### B. A. Mahadeva Sastri, *The Bhagavad-Gita with the commentary of Sri Sankaracharya* (2nd ed., 1901)
- **Edition used:** "SECOND EDITION, REVISED AND IMPROVED, WITH ADDITIONAL NOTES", Mysore, 1901 (printed at the
  G. T. A. Printing Works), series "Vedic Religion" (1st ed. 1897).
- **Scan:** archive.org `{MS_ID}` (University of California Libraries copy). Text from the archive.org ABBYY hOCR,
  merged word-by-word with our tesseract 5 OCR of the PDF (tessdata_best `eng`); body pages = PDF pp. ~35–505.
- **Included:** Sastri's translation of each verse (`translation`); his translation of Shankara's bhashya as
  paragraphs (`commentary`); the bhashya passage that leads into a verse (`introduction`, stored on the verse it
  introduces: Shankara's chapter openings, "(Question) … (Answer) …" lead-ins, "The Lord says:"). Sastri's bracketed
  remarks inside the body text are kept (they are part of his rendering).
- **Excluded:** footnotes (smaller type; mostly Anandagiri's gloss "(A.)" and Sastri's notes), Sastri's italic
  side-headings (used only as boundaries), running heads, page numbers, footnote reference marks.
- **Typography:** the original prints long vowels with circumflexes (Brâhmana) and retroflex/palatal consonants
  in italics (Brâhma*n*a, *s*ruti). Neither survives OCR, so Sanskrit words appear in plain letters (Brahmana,
  sruti, gunas). OCR garbles of italic letters ("gu«as", "Brahma7;a", "sa;«sara") are repaired only when exactly one
  word of the book's own clean vocabulary / our DB lemma list fits; otherwise the token is left untouched and
  listed (`translation_unreadable_words`) or counted (`commentary_confidence`).
- **Licence:** public domain (published 1897/1901; Sastri d. 1926).

### How confidence is assigned
- **Swarupananda word-for-word.** Each gloss pair's headword is mapped to our DCS word list (`words`), allowing
  compound spans and two-word groups. `dev` = the scan's Devanagari (five OCR readings: our eng+san run on smoothed
  and on plain images, the DLI OCR, san-only and Devanagari-only runs) matches the DB form; `gloss` = matched via
  gloss vs. DB meaning or transliteration (Kshetra ↔ kṣetram); `elimination` = the last remaining verse word;
  `supplied` = a word the translator adds in parentheses, e.g. "(तु) but", read identically by two OCRs;
  `unresolved` = gloss kept but headword unidentified (`term_dev` null). The headword printed is always our DB form,
  never an OCR guess; compounds are hyphenated like `word_meanings` (कर्म-फल-हेतुः). **high**: nothing unresolved,
  ≤1 guessed, no residual non-word in the glosses, ≤1 DB word unglossed, no weak Devanagari match. **medium**: ≤1
  unresolved, guesses (incl. Devanagari matches scoring < 0.75) ≤ 15 % of the pairs, ≤1 non-word, ≤3 unglossed DB
  words. Both tiers also require no implausible gloss (empty, starting with punctuation, unbalanced brackets, or a
  lone function word such as "of" for a content word — typical of a mis-segmented pair). **low**: everything else.
  Spot checks of `medium` still find occasional segmentation slips (e.g. a compound glossed once by the book but
  split over two DB words, "निःश्रेयस … करौ"); `high` verses checked were clean. Treat `medium` as "proofread before
  publishing" and `low` (in the `_low` file) as raw material only.
  The book itself omits glosses for some case endings and particles (Preface), so a few unglossed DB words are normal.
- **Swarupananda rendering / comments.** high = every word agrees with the sacred-texts transcription after OCR
  repair; medium = the 1909 print has other real words there (edition variant; 1909 kept); low = residual OCR
  non-words.
- **Mahadeva Sastri.** translation: high = no unreadable word, medium = one, low = more. commentary: high ≤ 0.5 %
  unreadable tokens, medium ≤ 2 %, low otherwise. `confidence` = the lower of the two.

## 5. Known limitations
- Swarupananda's Devanagari headwords are reconstructed from our DB, not transcribed: where the book segments
  differently from DCS (a compound as one word, a different sandhi split) the DB form is shown. The English
  `meaning` is the book's, with only OCR repairs (witness-confirmed) and line-break de-hyphenation.
- Swarupananda numbering = ours except chapter 13 (the book omits Arjuna's question; book 13.n = our 13.n+1) and
  Discourse I §27/§29 (book §27 = our 27 + first half of 28; §29 = second half of 28 + 29).
- Swarupananda renderings/comments carry the book's circumflexes (Brâhmana) where the sacred-texts proofreading
  confirms the word; glosses carry them only for proper names spelt that way elsewhere in the same book.
- Sastri: plain-letter Sanskrit (see above). A few badly printed pages (e.g. Discourse X, verses 37–41) remain
  noisy after the two-engine merge and are marked low.
